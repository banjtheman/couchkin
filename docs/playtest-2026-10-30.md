A passive local monitor inspected connection metadata, state transitions and companion captions; it did not record audio or evaluate visual correctness or audible interruption timing.

- Live connected at **18:11:54.042 UTC** (2:11:54 PM EDT).
- The resumed monitor began at **18:13:02 UTC**.
- Background observations failed at **18:16:24.353** and **18:18:01.424 UTC**. Voice replies continued. The old log retained only `observation_failed`; it cannot distinguish the failed stage or reason.
- Live reported `invalid_request_error` at **18:19:05.212** and **18:19:21.859 UTC**. Replies continued afterward. The old log did not retain rejected-command correlation or detailed error categories.
- At **18:24:35.792 UTC** (2:24:35 PM EDT), the voice socket closed unexpectedly with **1006**, `connection_lost`, and `intentional:false`. Couchkin paused playback, microphone sending and automatic work. The user saw the retained failure message.
- The connected interval was **12 minutes 41.750 seconds**. No OBS/overlay loss, user pause, `audio_backpressure`, or documented expiry event was recorded during that interval. No clean `session.closed` finalization arrived, so final API usage remains unconfirmed.
- The user requested a pause for investigation; the monitor was stopped and its recurring automation paused. Private evidence remains in ignored `tmp/` files. It is not included in this repository.

The evidence establishes an unexpected connection loss, **not its root cause**. The two earlier invalid requests occurred over five minutes before the disconnect; there is no evidence that they caused it. This change does not claim to fix that transport failure or silently reconnect paid sessions.

## Why visual context could trail

The retained state transitions contained 53 observation intervals. Median time between observation starts was **11.01 seconds**; median observed duration was **2.28 seconds**, with a maximum of **4.29 seconds**. These are application observation intervals, not measurements of speech latency. The periodic loop also deferred work while Pip was speaking or the conversation backend was busy.

There was no speech-triggered refresh. An overloaded timestamp represented both capture time and polling attempts, allowing an unsuccessful or unchanged poll to make a cached frame appear newer. Typed requests could reuse frames up to 30 seconds old. Returned summaries had no explicit age limit.

## Changes prepared after pausing

- The first recognized user transcript fragment after a speech gap triggers a quiet, priority observation. Newer speech replaces older observation work; there is at most one observer request in progress.
- Speech refreshes never request unsolicited commentary. They can start while the user is still talking and while the conversation backend is busy.
- Per-frame capture timestamps are separate from attempt timing. They use OBS request time, conservatively including response delay, rather than claiming an exact game-engine timestamp. Cached images for typed requests expire after two seconds, summaries after six seconds, and unsolicited reactions after four seconds.
- Instructions require fresh evidence for questions about current cards, targets, numbers, or changed scenes.
- Private diagnostics retain bounded, allowlisted transport and observation metadata so a later failure can be localized without saving conversation content.

The Live voice frontend does not accept images directly: a vision-capable backend interprets images and returns text context. Transcript fragments can trigger work early, but neither context submission nor its acknowledgment proves the model used it before replying. See [visual context and early transcript processing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-delegation#add-images-and-visual-context) and [context delivery timing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-conversations#understand-when-context-reaches-the-model).

These changes need another human gameplay rehearsal to establish perceived responsiveness and sustained reliability. No new paid session was started during implementation.

## Second run: speech-triggered context

The user started another Slay the Spire 2 rehearsal with build `ef0ba37`, Blue Snowball and the existing OBS setup. Live connected at **19:03:23.682 UTC** and the user paused at **19:12:11.062 UTC**. The connection closed cleanly at **19:12:11.744 UTC**, approximately **8 minutes 48 seconds** after connecting. No new unexpected voice or OBS disconnect and no transport diagnostic were recorded in that interval. This is shorter than the earlier run that failed; it does not establish sustained reliability or resolve the earlier 1006 cause.

The monitor recorded **51 speech-triggered observations**: 47 submitted context, three superseded by newer work, and one failed to parse a model response. The failed observation was at **19:07:56.991 UTC**; subsequent observations submitted normally. Median capture-to-submission duration among the 47 submitted speech observations was **2.012 seconds**, with a maximum of **3.090 seconds**. There were also 27 periodic observations: 17 submissions and ten superseded. No stale outcomes were recorded. These are local context-submission measurements, not model-use acknowledgments, audible response latency or proof of visual correctness.

Several companion caption groups contained repeated “Mm” / “Mm-hmm” filler. Captions alone cannot establish how those sounded or whether they interrupted the streamer. The user reported that Pip was **great at following along**, but felt more like a helpful buddy than a teasing friend. They requested stronger, selectable personalities and ready-made companions. That human feedback informs the next iteration; no automated quality score is inferred from the captions.

The user ended the run. The passive monitor was stopped, its recurring automation deleted, and the local cursor marked complete. Private evidence stays in ignored `tmp/`; no audio, game screenshots or user transcripts are included in this report.
